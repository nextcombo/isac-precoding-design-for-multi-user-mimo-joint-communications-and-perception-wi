# ISAC: Precoding Design for Multi-User MIMO Joint Communications and Perception with Python Code

This paper investigates the precoding design of multi-user (MU) multiple input multiple output (MIMO) joint communication and perception (JCAS) systems, taking into account potential interference between the perception and communication channels. For general input signals, we derive performance metrics for perception and communication, namely detection probability and Signal to Interference plus Noise Ratio (SINR). The study shows that by utilizing communication signals for perception, performance loss in communication can be avoided when channel interference occurs. However, the peak kurtosis of the alphabet used for transmitting communication signals imposes a limitation on perception performance. Simulation results are also provided under different configurations.
Introduction: Multi-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems are fundamental in modern wireless communications, offering partial compensation for increased path loss through beamforming in high frequency bands. MIMO enables spatial multiplexing, which utilizes spatial diversity to simultaneously transmit multiple data streams, thereby enhancing spectral efficiency. At the same time, Joint Communications and Perception (JCAS) has attracted significant attention due to its potential to support various new functionalities, such as collision risk warnings for vehicles at intersections. In JCAS systems, the precision of perception is closely related to the available bandwidth, making high frequency bands an area of research focus for perception applications.
The core challenge in JCAS lies in designing transmission signals that can effectively support both communication and perception. While some studies have proposed precoding algorithms for multi-user MIMO JCAS systems, the design of transmission signals remains a hot topic in this field. Some studies employ phase modulated communication signals to illuminate perception targets, achieving maximum signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) for target detection in single-input-single-output systems. However, traditional communication systems often use higher order quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) formats to improve throughput, which leads to a decrease in perception performance. To achieve a flexible balance between communication and perception performance, some studies propose using constellation shaping technology in JCAS systems.
Existing research has explored the performance trade-offs in MIMO systems, with many focusing on metrics such as sidelobe levels and image signal-to-noise ratio, which are related to average detection probability or Cramér-Rao bounds. Additionally, these studies typically employ a common constellation format for communication signals and rely solely on communication signals or dedicated sensing signals for perception. This paper supplements existing research by allowing for different constellation formats for communication signals and directly optimizing detection rates.
This paper thoroughly analyzes the advantages and disadvantages of using joint signaling or dedicated signaling for perception, explores different application scenarios, derives two performance indicators: target detection rate of power detector and communication signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio, and finally studies the impact of interference generated by perception signals on communication systems.
Method Introduction 1
System Model Construction: Establish a single-carrier, single-site multi-user MIMO JCAS system model. The base station is equipped with both transmitters and receivers, utilizing an evenly spaced uniform linear array with half-wavelength antenna spacing. This setup facilitates simultaneous single-target perception detection and spatial multiplexing communication with multiple single-antenna users. Modeling efforts are directed towards the transmitter, communication channel (Rayleigh fading), perception channel (Swerling 1 model), communication receiver (MMSE equalization, LLR output), and perception receiver. The perception receiver employs a constant false alarm power detector based on the Neyman-Pearson criterion to achieve target detection.
2  
Derived Performance Metrics: For perceptual performance, closed-form expressions for detection probability were derived through methods such as moment generation functions and eigenvalue decomposition. It was analyzed that the detection probability is determined by the kurtosis of the signal combination emitted, with minimizing kurtosis maximizing detection probability. The optimal strategy is to illuminate the perception target with signals having the lowest kurtosis. For communication performance, a formula for the communication signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) was derived. It was found that while interference from the perception target introduces phase randomness, on average it enhances the SINR when the communication signal is reused as the perception signal.
3  
Design Simulation Scheme: Employ a self-encoder framework with integrated neural networks to achieve pre-coding and demodulation. Regulate the proportion of the perceptual task in total loss through weight parameters, designing a joint loss function that includes communication loss and detection loss. To encourage the algorithm to converge to the global optimal detection probability, initialize the pre-coding matrix to its state with maximum kurtosis. Set simulation parameters such as dual users, specific perception angle range, and an uneven linear array with 16 antennas, comparing the performance of three perception schemes: dedicated constant modulus perception signals, 16-QAM communication signals, and combined constant modulus signals with communication signals.
Conclusion This paper provides design guidance for the transmission signal of MIMO JCAS system based on the interference characteristics between perception and communication channels. The perception interest area can be divided into two categories: those that interfere with communication users and those that do not. Different treatment strategies are adopted: when there is no interference, using a constant modulus signal for perception achieves optimal target detection effects without affecting communication performance; when there is interference, the system needs to make performance trade-offs. Utilizing communication signals for perception helps improve communication performance, but the perception performance is limited by the kurtosis of the used communication signals; it is not recommended to use combinations of different signals for perception, as this approach results in inferior target detection effects compared to single-signal perception schemes. The research findings of this paper also confirm the conclusion that the kurtosis of communication transmission signals is a core performance balancing parameter for JCAS systems, aligning with existing research.
